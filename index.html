How to assess

Observe the mother carefully during your visit. Tap all symptoms you notice. The app will calculate her risk level automatically.

Symptoms Observed

Persistent low mood or sadness

Mother seems sad, tearful, or hopeless most of the time

Loss of interest in baby or activities

No joy in caring for baby or things she used to enjoy

Sleep problems (not baby-related)

Cannot sleep even when baby sleeps, or sleeps too much

Extreme fatigue or low energy

Unusual tiredness that rest does not help

Appetite changes

Significant weight loss/gain, not eating or overeating

Feelings of worthlessness or guilt

Says she is a "bad mother" or feels excessive guilt

Difficulty concentrating

Cannot focus, make decisions, or remember things

Excessive anxiety or panic

Worries constantly about baby's health or her own

Thoughts of self-harm or harming baby

URGENT: Any mention of suicide or hurting the baby — select immediately

Lack of family support

Family conflict, domestic issues, or no help at home

About MatriCare

MatriCare helps ASHA workers track postpartum mothers and identify signs of postpartum depression (PPD) early. Early detection saves lives.

How to Register a Mother

  • Tap "Register New Mother" on the dashboard
  • Fill in her name, age, village, and delivery details
  • Tap "Save & Register"
  • The mother will appear in your list immediately

How to Record a Visit

  • Open the mother's profile from the list
  • Tap "Record New Visit"
  • Select all symptoms you observed
  • Add any notes about the visit
  • Tap "Save Visit" — the app calculates risk automatically

Understanding Risk Levels

  • Low Risk (0-2 symptoms): Normal follow-up schedule
  • Moderate Risk (3-5 symptoms): Visit weekly, monitor closely
  • High Risk (6+ symptoms or harm thoughts): Refer to doctor immediately

Exporting Data

Tap "Export" on the dashboard to download all mother records as a CSV file. This can be shared with your supervisor or uploaded to the health management system.

Remember: This app is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Always refer high-risk mothers to a qualified doctor.
