02 / Human Need

Awareness × Expression

Not every health need that exists becomes an expressed demand. The distinction between latent and apparent needs helps identify where behavioral intervention is required.

Latent
Apparent / Expressed
Unaware
Unaware + Latent

The person does not recognize the problem or need.

Unaware + Apparent

The problem is observable, but its meaning or cause is not recognized.

Aware
Aware + Latent

The person knows about the issue but has not activated behavior.

Aware + Apparent

The person recognizes the problem and actively expresses a need or intent.

04 / Behavioral Diagnosis

COM-B Diagnostic Model

When a desired health behavior is not happening, diagnose the missing component before designing an intervention.

C

Capability

Does the person have the knowledge, skills, physical capacity and psychological ability to perform the behavior?

Intervention examples:

Education · Training · Skill building · Planning

O

Opportunity

Does the physical and social environment make the behavior possible, accessible and socially supported?

Intervention examples:

Access · Convenience · Infrastructure · Social support

M

Motivation

Does the person have enough reflective and automatic motivation to perform the behavior?

Intervention examples:

Goals · Feedback · Rewards · Identity · Emotional relevance

07 / Design Principles

What Health Behavior Design Should Optimize

Reduce friction Make the desired action easier than the competing behavior.
Create immediate value Don't rely only on distant health benefits.
Build self-efficacy Create small achievable successes.
Design the environment Change context instead of relying entirely on willpower.
Respect autonomy Support informed choices rather than coercion.
Match readiness Different stages require different interventions.
Use feedback Make progress visible and understandable.
Design for maintenance The real goal is sustained behavior, not a single action.
