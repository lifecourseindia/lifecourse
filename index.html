BEHAVIOUR DIAGNOSIS

Awareness × Expression

Knowing that a problem exists does not automatically mean the person is ready to act.

LATENT
APPARENT / EXPRESSED
UNAWARE
Unaware + LatentThe problem is not recognised and a need is not actively expressed.
Unaware + ApparentThe behaviour is visible to others although the person may not recognise it.
AWARE
Aware + LatentThe person knows the problem exists but has not translated awareness into action.
Aware + ApparentThe problem is recognised and the need for change is clearly expressed.

BEHAVIOUR DIAGNOSTIC ENGINE

COM-B

Before designing an intervention, diagnose what is preventing the behaviour.

C

Capability

Does the person have the psychological and physical capability, knowledge and skills required?

O

Opportunity

Does the physical and social environment make the behaviour possible and accessible?

M

Motivation

Do automatic and reflective processes provide enough motivation to act?

INTERACTIVE DIAGNOSTIC

What is blocking the behaviour?

A simple starting point for identifying the dominant behavioural barrier.

1. Does the person know what to do?

2. Do they have the skills/resources?

3. Does the environment support the behaviour?

4. Do they genuinely want to change?

5. Is there a reliable prompt or cue?

HABIT SCIENCE

From deliberate action to automatic behaviour

Repeated behaviours become easier when connected to stable cues and reinforcement.

👁

Cue

A signal tells the person that the behaviour should happen.

⚡

Action

The person performs the target behaviour.

🎯

Reward

Feedback reinforces the behaviour.

🔁

Repetition

Repeated behaviour can become increasingly automatic.

BEHAVIOUR DESIGN

Principles for designing health behaviour

Move from telling people what they should do toward designing conditions in which behaviour can happen.

Reduce frictionMake the desired behaviour easier and more accessible.
Make it visibleUse cues and prompts at the moment of decision.
Build capabilityTeach skills and create confidence, not just awareness.
Use social proofMake supportive norms visible.
Design for identityConnect behaviour with who people believe they are.
Create feedbackHelp people see progress before distant health outcomes arrive.
Start smallReduce the initial behavioural barrier.
Design the environmentChange defaults, availability and context.
